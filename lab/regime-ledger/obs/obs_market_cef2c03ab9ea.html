What Changed

  1. All three required indexes fell sharply versus November 13, with Nasdaq the weakest.
  2. S&P 1500 decliners outnumbered advancers by more than two to one.
  3. Consumer staples and utilities were the only positive sector ETF groups in the sourced sector board.
  4. Cap-weight S&P 500 underperformed equal-weight substantially, showing concentrated mega-cap pressure alongside broad selling.
  5. Treasury yields eased during the equity decline, partially contradicting a simple rising-rate selloff narrative.

Risks and Contradictions

A key contradiction is that the broad market was weak while equal-weight held up better than cap-weight; this is both genuine negative breadth and concentrated technology pressure, not one or the other. VIX closing levels disagree materially among published sources and are excluded from scoring. Daily credit spreads, complete sector multihorizon ranks, official exchange-wide advance-decline totals, index moving-average levels and verified current-quarter AI cash-flow/capex comparisons were not obtained. These limitations reduce confidence, even though enough same-session equity and cross-market evidence exists to score the five components.

Educational Summary

Broad declines accompanied by weak participation, underperforming growth leadership and relative strength in defensive sectors typically characterize a defensive equity environment. A subsequent improvement in breadth, relative strength and risk confirmation would challenge this assessment; a further deterioration in credit or intermediate trends would strengthen it. This is a description of observed market conditions, not an action recommendation.
