What Changed

Fed held, but with two hawkish dissents. The October 28 FOMC maintained 3.75%–4.00%, describing economic growth as strong and inflation as elevated. Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack favored a 25bp increase; Chair Kevin Warsh did not pre-commit to December. The decision preserved policy state but sharpened the contrast between a hold today and the market's still-high December hike expectations. Source: Federal Reserve October 28 2 p.m. EDT statement and Reuters October 28 post-decision reporting, https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm and https://www.reuters.com/business/fed-likely-go-into-wait-and-see-mode-rates-2026-10-28/ .

Treasury curve steepened and real yields rose. Official October 28 10-year nominal par yield 5.05%, versus 4.99% October 27 (+6bp); 10-year real 2.67%, versus 2.64% (+3bp). The 2-year nominal was 4.67%, versus 4.70% (−3bp). This is a long-end headwind for non-yielding metals even as short rates eased. Source: U.S. Treasury October 27–28 official curves, URLs in Weighted Score.

Spot metal divergence persisted after the decision. Ausecure's matched October 28 5 p.m. EDT spot prices show gold $4,067.29 (+$6.96/+0.17%), silver $53.97 (−$0.45/−0.83%). The current spot GSR rose to 75.36; that is a warning of gold leadership, not proof of a final ratio high. Ausecure's price snapshot is after the 4 p.m. equity close, so ETF and metal daily returns are not synchronized to the same cutoff. Source: Ausecure October 28, https://www.ausecure.com/gold-market-close-oct-28-2026/ .

Miners held up better than silver metal. All four October 28 4 p.m. EDT ETF regular-session closes rose roughly 0.7%–1.0%. Positive miner breadth is constructive but cannot yet override the week's spot silver underperformance or restore October 16 miner levels. Source: StockAnalysis/S&P Global Market Intelligence October 28 historical pages, URLs in Regime Snapshot.

Alternative Opportunity

No superior liquid risk-on replacement for metals is established by this evidence. Short-duration Treasury bills/cash-like instruments remain a more defensible capital-preservation alternative while metals confirmation is incomplete, although reinvestment and inflation risks persist. The official U.S. Treasury October 28 3-month par yield 4.18% is an indicative curve rate, not an executable T-bill auction yield or a guaranteed investment return. Long-duration nominal Treasuries are not an automatically superior substitute given 10-year yields rising to 5.05% and the Treasury's projected Q4 borrowing needs.
