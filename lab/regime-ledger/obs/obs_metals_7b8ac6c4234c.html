Macro / Flow / Positioning Evidence

Monetary policy and real yields

Scored status: UNAVAILABLE.

Reuters reported August CPI up 0.4% month over month and Fed-hike odds around 87% for the September meeting. The nominal 10-year Treasury yield briefly reached 4.9915% before easing.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-11.

The U.S. Treasury's official real-yield table showed September 10 observations but had not yet posted September 11 values at report time. The September 10 10-year real yield was 2.55%, but it is stale for the September 11 scoring session and is not used in the score.

Source: U.S. Department of the Treasury, accessed 2026-09-12.

U.S. dollar

Reuters reported the dollar index broadly flat on Friday after strengthening earlier in the week; another Reuters currency report noted the index had been near 99.08 entering the session.

Sources: Reuters, 2026-09-11.

The currency impulse is therefore mildly negative for metals rather than strongly adverse: the dollar is not surging, but it is no longer providing the clear tailwind seen earlier in the month.

ETF and fund flows

The World Gold Council's August report remains structurally strong: global gold ETFs attracted $18 billion, the second-largest monthly inflow on record, lifting holdings by 121 tonnes to a record 4,189 tonnes.

Source: World Gold Council, 2026-09-09.

However, the latest weekly commodity-fund data weakened materially. Reuters/LSEG reported $537 million of outflows from gold and precious-metals funds in the week ending September 9.

Source: Reuters/LSEG Lipper, 2026-09-11.

That shift prevents the ETF/fund-flow component from receiving the near-maximum score warranted by August alone.

Central-bank and reserve demand

World Gold Council data showed central banks bought a net 23 tonnes in July, with China buying 20 tonnes and Poland 8 tonnes. Reported year-to-date central-bank purchases were about 130 tonnes.

Source: World Gold Council, 2026-09-03.

China subsequently reported additional August buying, extending its accumulation streak. Official-sector demand remains one of the strongest structural supports for gold.

Source: People's Bank of China / SAFE data reported September 2026.

CFTC positioning

The latest official weekly positioning dataset retrievable at report time showed gold speculative length still elevated but below late-August levels, while silver positioning remained materially less crowded.

The latest fully verified gold managed-money observation available in the retrieved data was 136,771 net-long contracts as of September 1, down 7,976 week over week. Because no newer verified CFTC table was retrievable at report time, this weekly series is treated as the latest available CFTC observation, not as a daily carry-forward.

Source: CFTC official dataset, latest retrievable report at report time.

Physical supply and demand

Gold physical demand remains mixed. World Gold Council data show strong investment and official demand alongside healthy mine supply; Reuters also reported subdued Indian physical demand during the September 11 rebound.

Sources: World Gold Council Gold Demand Trends Q2 2026; Reuters, 2026-09-11.

The Silver Institute expects a sixth consecutive annual silver-market deficit in 2026, forecasting a 46.3 million ounce shortfall with mine production broadly flat. High prices are encouraging thrifting and substitution in photovoltaics and weaker jewelry/silverware demand, which temper the bullish physical signal.

Source: Silver Institute World Silver Survey 2026.

Fiscal and Treasury financing

Long-end Treasury stress remains part of the metals backdrop. Reuters reported the 10-year Treasury yield briefly reaching 4.9915% on September 11 before easing. Separately, Treasury's enlarged buyback program has not prevented yields from testing multi-year highs.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-11.

The fiscal impulse remains two-sided: high debt and financing stress support gold's strategic appeal, while the associated term premium and elevated yields are tactically negative.

Action Implied

The independent evidence supports remaining in correction while acknowledging Friday stabilization.

The case against an immediate bull-trend upgrade is straightforward: gold and silver both lost ground for the week, Fed-hike odds rose to roughly 87%, nominal yields tested 5%, and the latest weekly precious-metals fund-flow data turned negative.

The case against a more bearish secular interpretation is also strong: record gold ETF holdings, continued central-bank accumulation, fiscal stress, and a persistent silver deficit remain intact.

A return to bull-trend would require a more durable price recovery accompanied by easing policy/yield pressure and, under v0.3, verifiable miner confirmation. Continued rejection below the August gold reference with restrictive rates would keep the correction call in place.
