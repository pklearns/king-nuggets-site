Risks and Contradictions

Evidence supporting Defensive: four consecutive S&P declines; 2-to-1 negative S&P breadth; Nasdaq new lows nearly five times new highs; Russell underperformance; equal-weight underperformance; nine of 11 sectors lower; technology weakness; Brent above $107; WTI above $102; the 10-year near 5%; the 30-year above 5.3%; and rising volatility.

Evidence against treating this as a broken primary bear trend: the S&P is still less than 3% below its August record and remains up roughly 11% for 2026; the Nasdaq and Russell also remain positive year to date; and the current decline has not yet produced high absolute volatility comparable with major stress episodes. Source: Reuters and Associated Press, 2026-09-10.

The principal contradiction is:

Primary price structure remains broadly positive, but almost every current short- and intermediate-horizon confirmation measure is defensive.

That contradiction does not invalidate the Defensive regime call. The framework is intended to identify the current market environment before a long-term trend break becomes obvious in the headline indexes.

Current conclusion

DEFENSIVE — High confidence — Deteriorating

The most important feature is no longer any one index decline. It is the synchronization of weak breadth, weaker small-cap/equal-weight performance, narrowing leadership, rising oil, rising Treasury yields and rising volatility.

A move back toward Neutral would normally require breadth to repair, Nasdaq new lows to contract materially, small caps and equal weight to stop lagging, and the oil/rates shock to stabilize. Until then, the current evidence supports maintaining the Defensive regime classification independently of the still-positive primary trend.
