Educational Summary

Friday improved the short-term market picture but did not fully reverse the deterioration that developed Wednesday and Thursday.

The week contained two distinct environments. Early in the week, falling oil and yields combined with powerful technology leadership to push the Nasdaq to records. Midweek, Treasury yields surged above 5%, breadth deteriorated sharply, and small caps weakened. Friday then provided partial relief: oil declined and the major indexes recovered, but Treasury yields remained historically elevated and Russell 2000 barely participated.

The horizon split is therefore:

Primary trend: positive in large-cap equities.
Intermediate condition: mixed.
Short-term condition: stabilizing after deterioration.
Relative strength: Nasdaq and large-cap growth leading; Russell and smaller companies lagging.

Current conclusion

NEUTRAL — Moderate confidence — Stabilizing

The improvement is meaningful but incomplete. Friday demonstrated that large-cap equities can still attract demand when oil pressure subsides, and the S&P remains close to its record. At the same time, the market has not resolved its central divergence: strong large-cap index structure against weak small-cap relative performance and unusually high long-term interest rates.

A more convincing Risk-On environment would normally require improved completed-session breadth, better equal-weight participation, stronger Russell relative performance, and some relief in long-term Treasury yields. A move toward Defensive would require the current weakness underneath the indexes to begin breaking the still-positive large-cap primary trends or to receive clearer confirmation from volatility and credit.
