Tactical and Secular Probabilities

Tactical 1--6 Months: Bull 20% / Base 43% / Bear 37%.

The tactical distribution improved at the margin because the labor report materially reduced the probability of an October hike. However, gold reversed an early gain of more than 1%, silver also finished lower, DXY remained elevated, and the 10-year real yield stayed near 3%. The evidence supports stabilization inside a correction, not a renewed bull trend.

Secular 12--24 Months: Bull 54% / Base 31% / Bear 15%.

The secular case remains moderately positive. Central-bank reserve diversification, strong gold-ETF holdings, fiscal-financing stress, and silver's structural supply deficit remain supportive. The principal counterweight is an unusually high real-rate regime that has persisted longer than a simple cyclical pullback would imply.

What Changed

The September employment report was the dominant new catalyst. Nonfarm payrolls increased 29,000, versus 90,000 expected, unemployment rose to 4.2%, and wage growth slowed. Source: Reuters, 2026-10-02.

Markets responded by reducing the probability of an October Fed hike to roughly 22%, from around 70% earlier in the week. Source: Reuters/CME FedWatch, 2026-10-02.

Gold initially gained more than 1% after the jobs report but reversed to $4,140.06/oz by 2:33 p.m. EDT. U.S. gold futures settled at $4,162.30/oz. Spot silver fell to $60.36/oz. Source: Reuters, 2026-10-02.

The Treasury curve did not validate a durable easing impulse. The official October 2 print showed the 10-year nominal yield at 5.28%, up 4 basis points on the day, and the 10-year real yield at 2.92%. Source: U.S. Treasury data, 2026-10-02, as reproduced by Arcane Intel.
