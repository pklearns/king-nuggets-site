Daily Market Regime Watch

Latest completed U.S. session: Thursday, September 3, 2026

Regime Snapshot

Regime: Neutral
Confidence: Moderate-High
Direction of change: Improving materially; immediate Defensive risk receded

The S&P 500 rose 1.06% to 7,747.71, the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.40% to 26,584.06, and the Dow advanced 1.18% to 53,686.11. The rally followed Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller's comments that he would support holding rates steady if incoming inflation data continue to cool, cutting market-implied odds of a September rate hike to roughly 50% from 63% the prior day.

Participation was constructive for a second straight session: NYSE advancers led decliners 1.98-to-1 and Nasdaq advancers 1.67-to-1. NYSE new highs also exceeded new lows 284 to 186, and S&P 500 new highs exceeded lows 14 to 8. The principal internal warning remains Nasdaq's 71 new highs versus 116 new lows, while equal-weight S&P gained only about 0.6% versus 1.06% for the cap-weighted index.

Interpretation: The market moved decisively away from the Defensive threshold reached earlier this week. The regime remains Neutral, rather than Risk-On, because the rebound is still partly megacap-led, Nasdaq high/low breadth has not repaired, and oil remains near $95 Brent despite relief in Treasury yields.

S&P 500: The index closed at 7,747.71, only modestly below its August record. Two consecutive advancing sessions, improved exchange breadth and lower yields repair much of the immediate damage from August 28-September 1. The primary trend remains positive.

Nasdaq Composite: Nasdaq led at +1.40%, supported by megacap technology and software. Nvidia gained 1.8%, Snowflake surged 16.6%, and ServiceNow, Salesforce and Adobe rose between roughly 2% and 6.5%. The important contradiction is that Nasdaq still recorded more new lows than new highs, so price leadership remains stronger than underlying high/low participation.

Russell 2000: Small caps participated positively in the rate-relief rally, extending Wednesday's stabilization. A sufficiently reliable final Russell 2000 closing level was not available in the sources reviewed, so the exact close is intentionally left unquoted rather than inferred.

Leadership and Rotation

Thursday's leadership shifted back toward large-cap growth, software, consumer discretionary and financials, while the prior inflationary leadership in energy paused.

The move was not purely narrow: NYSE and Nasdaq advance-decline breadth were both clearly positive. However, equal weight lagged cap weight, with RSP up about 0.6% versus the S&P 500's 1.06%, indicating that megacap growth contributed disproportionately.

Durable leadership: the longer-horizon picture remains mixed. Energy and healthcare entered the session with the strongest recent trend breadth, while value had held an intermediate participation advantage. Thursday improved growth leadership substantially, but one rate-relief session does not yet establish a durable factor reversal.

Leading Sectors and Subsectors

1. Consumer Discretionary — session leader

Consumer discretionary was the largest percentage gainer among the 11 S&P 500 sectors, rising about 1.6% in final sector reporting.

Status: Strong one-day leadership. The sector's broader intermediate breadth had been weak earlier this week, so this is best classified as repair rather than established durable leadership.

2. Software — strongest identifiable industry leadership

Snowflake jumped 16.6% after strong revenue guidance. ServiceNow, Salesforce and Adobe gained roughly 2.1% to 6.5%, producing a broad software rebound.

Status: Meaningful short-term leadership with earnings confirmation; durability remains to be proven.

3. Megacap growth / AI platforms

The Magnificent Seven group materially supported the Nasdaq. Nvidia rose 1.8% after announcing a $12.9 billion acquisition of Hugging Face.

Status: Reasserted short-term leadership, but still concentrated.

4. Financials / trading-sensitive names

Financials participated in the rally, while Robinhood rose 16.6%, Coinbase 10.1%, and Strategy 17.6% as risk appetite improved.

Status: Positive risk-appetite confirmation, though much of the strongest performance was company- and crypto-beta-specific.

Lagging Sectors and Subsectors

1. Energy — session laggard

Energy was the weakest S&P sector Thursday. Brent crude nevertheless remained elevated near $95.52 and WTI closed around $91.30.

Status: One-day laggard within a still-strong longer-term trend. The sector's relative weakness reflects rate-relief rotation rather than a confirmed structural breakdown.

2. Semiconductors — mixed / internally weak

Broadcom fell 2.7% after issuing a weaker-than-expected revenue forecast, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index was reported lower during the session even as the broader technology sector advanced.

Status: Mixed. AI-platform and software strength did not translate into uniform semiconductor leadership.

A complete authoritative final ranking of all 11 S&P sectors beyond the top and bottom groups was not available, so lower-order rankings are not forced.

Breadth and Internals

Thursday's final exchange breadth was constructive:

  • NYSE: advancers led decliners 1.98-to-1
  • Nasdaq: 2,957 advancers vs. 1,771 decliners, or 1.67-to-1 positive
  • NYSE new highs/lows: 284 / 186
  • S&P 500 new highs/lows: 14 / 8
  • Nasdaq new highs/lows: 71 / 116
  • U.S. exchange volume: 15.25 billion shares, slightly above the 20-day average of 15.09 billion

This is the second consecutive session of positive advance-decline participation and is a meaningful improvement from the 2.8-to-1 negative breadth recorded Tuesday.

The high/low structure is less fully repaired. NYSE and S&P high/low breadth turned positive, but Nasdaq new lows still exceeded new highs by roughly 1.6-to-1.

Equal weight also lagged: RSP gained about 0.6%, versus 1.06% for the cap-weighted S&P. That means breadth improved broadly at the exchange level, but the S&P's largest companies still contributed disproportionately to the headline advance.

Reliable September 3 closing percentages of S&P 500, Nasdaq and Russell constituents above their 20-, 50- and 200-day moving averages were not available from a sufficiently trustworthy current source. The latest verified September 2 readings remained below 50% above the 50-day average across all three universes, so moving-average breadth is treated as stale / unscored for Thursday rather than silently carried forward.

Breadth grade: Positive daily participation / improving high-low structure / concentration still present.

Assessment: Positive confirmation. Volatility has moved further away from the level normally associated with a Defensive regime.

Credit

A fresh September 3 end-of-day high-yield spread observation was not yet available. Recent verified high-yield and Single-B spreads remained orderly rather than stressed.

Assessment: Slightly stale but still non-confirming of equity stress.

Treasury rates

The 2-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.33%, the 10-year to 4.76%, and the 30-year to roughly 5.24% after Waller's remarks reduced expectations for an imminent Fed hike.

Assessment: Material positive confirmation. Falling yields directly relieved one of the pressures that drove the regime toward Defensive earlier this week.

U.S. dollar

The dollar weakened broadly as U.S. yields fell and the Japanese yen strengthened.

Assessment: Mildly supportive through easier financial conditions.

Oil

Brent settled $95.52, down about 0.1%, while WTI rose 0.3% to $91.30.

Assessment: Still a material contradiction. The daily move was contained, but absolute oil prices remain high enough to keep inflation and consumer-pressure risks elevated.

What Changed

  1. Positive breadth persisted for a second session. NYSE and Nasdaq advancers again comfortably exceeded decliners.
  2. New-high/new-low structure improved materially. NYSE highs moved back above lows, and S&P highs exceeded lows, although Nasdaq lows still dominate.
  3. Rates reversed lower. The 10-year Treasury yield fell toward 4.76% and the probability of a September Fed hike dropped to about 50%.
  4. Growth leadership strengthened. Nasdaq led, software rallied sharply, and megacap technology regained momentum.
  5. Concentration remains unresolved. Equal-weight S&P rose only about 0.6%, materially less than cap weight, while Nasdaq new lows remained elevated.

Risks and Contradictions

Evidence supporting improvement: two consecutive sessions of positive advance-decline breadth; NYSE and S&P new highs exceeding new lows; S&P back near its record; Nasdaq leadership; VIX near 14.5; lower Treasury yields; softer dollar; and still-orderly credit.

Evidence preventing a Risk-On upgrade: Nasdaq new lows still exceed new highs; equal-weight S&P materially lagged cap weight; current moving-average breadth has not yet been verified back above 50% on the intermediate horizon; semiconductors were mixed; Brent remains near $95; and Friday's employment report can materially reprice Fed expectations again.

The central contradiction is:

Daily breadth, volatility and rates now look substantially healthier, while intermediate participation and the oil/inflation backdrop have not yet fully confirmed the price rebound.

Educational Summary

Thursday materially changes the balance of evidence compared with Tuesday.

The three-session sequence is now:

Tuesday: indexes ↓ + 2.8-to-1 negative breadth + new lows surge + oil/rates ↑ + VIX ↑
Wednesday: indexes ↑ + ~1.8-to-1 positive breadth + Russell leads + VIX ↓, but new lows remain elevated
Thursday: S&P +1.1% + Nasdaq +1.4% + positive breadth again + NYSE/S&P high-low structure improves + yields ↓ + VIX ↓

That is a meaningful stabilization sequence, not merely a one-day bounce.

The framework therefore moves away from the Defensive threshold reached Tuesday. It stops short of Risk-On because the strongest evidence of repair is still short-term: Nasdaq high/low breadth remains negative, equal weight lagged, and verified intermediate moving-average participation has not yet returned above 50%.

Current conclusion

NEUTRAL — Moderate-High confidence — Improving materially; immediate Defensive risk receded

The next confirmation step would normally be persistence: positive breadth after Friday's employment report, contraction in Nasdaq new lows, equal-weight participation closer to cap weight, and verified recovery in 50-day breadth.

The most important change Thursday is that rates, volatility, advance-decline breadth and price all improved together. Earlier this week those same dimensions were deteriorating together. That synchronization materially reduces the probability that the market is already in a sustained Defensive regime, while leaving the intermediate repair incomplete.
