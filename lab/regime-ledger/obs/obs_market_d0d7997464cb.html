Daily Market Regime Watch

Latest completed U.S. session: Friday, September 4, 2026

Regime Snapshot

Regime: Neutral
Confidence: Moderate-High
Direction of change: Mixed stabilization — Friday interrupted the rebound, but breadth repair and low stress measures remain intact

The S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,718.41, the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.29% to 26,506.99, and the Dow lost 0.51% to 53,413.60 after August payrolls rose by 162,000, nearly three times consensus. The stronger labor data lifted the market-implied probability of a September Fed hike to roughly 58%–61% and pushed Treasury yields higher. For the week, the large-cap indexes were essentially unchanged to modestly positive.

The headline decline did not produce a uniform breadth breakdown. NYSE decliners edged advancers by 1.04-to-1, while Nasdaq advancers actually led decliners 1.10-to-1. However, new lows still exceeded new highs on the NYSE, S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Broad U.S. moving-average participation improved to roughly 44.6% above the 20-day, 49.7% above the 50-day and 57.5% above the 200-day, but the 50-day reading remains just below a majority.

Interpretation: Friday reversed part of Thursday's rate-relief rally but did not recreate Tuesday's synchronized deterioration. Price remains near the highs, volatility is near 14, credit spreads remain tight, and broad moving-average participation improved. Against that, high/low breadth remains negative and the jobs report restored the rates/inflation constraint. The evidence remains best classified as Neutral.

S&P 500: The index gave back only part of Thursday's 1.06% gain and remains close to its August record. The primary price trend is intact, while the intermediate repair remains incomplete because S&P new lows exceeded new highs 6 to 3 Friday.

Nasdaq Composite: Nasdaq fell only 0.29% despite the rate shock. That relative resilience came from semiconductor strength: the PHLX Semiconductor Index gained 3.4%. The internal contradiction remains high/low breadth, with 61 new highs versus 99 new lows.

Russell 2000: Small caps held up better than the large-cap indexes. AP's closing index table reported the Russell 2000 +0.2% to 2,975.65, while some delayed index feeds showed a slightly different final print. Because of that feed discrepancy, the exact closing level is not used as a scored input. The useful signal is relative: small caps did not participate in Friday's large-cap decline to the same degree.

Leadership and Rotation

Friday produced a split between AI hardware / semiconductors and the broader growth complex.

Semiconductors were the clearest short-term leaders, gaining 3.4%, even as the S&P and Nasdaq declined. Sandisk, memory-related names and chip equipment stocks were among the strongest performers. This is meaningful short-term leadership, but the SOX remains about 17.8% lower for the quarter, so the group has not yet re-established durable intermediate leadership.

Industrials and the broad technology sector also posted modest gains. In contrast, consumer discretionary led sector losses, while software and services fell 2.1% despite remaining one of the stronger groups over the quarter.

The factor message is therefore not straightforwardly defensive: cyclical small caps held up, chips rallied and Nasdaq advance-decline breadth was positive. The pressure came primarily through rates, selected consumer names and software rather than indiscriminate de-risking.

Leading Sectors and Subsectors

1. Energy — durable structural leader

The freshest complete sector-breadth table available remains September 2, when approximately 95% of S&P energy stocks were above their 20-day average, 90% above the 50-day and 86% above the 200-day. Oil ended the week sharply higher amid renewed U.S.-Iran tensions.

Status: Durable structural leadership, although Friday's precise sector return was not sufficiently verified to rank it as the day's leader.

2. Technology / Semiconductors — Friday momentum leader

Technology posted a modest sector gain while semiconductors rose 3.4%.

Status: Strong short-term leadership. Intermediate durability remains unconfirmed because semiconductors are still down materially for the quarter.

3. Healthcare — structural breadth leader

The latest complete sector-breadth data, from September 2, showed approximately 69% above the 20-day, 81% above the 50-day and 81% above the 200-day.

Status: Durable participation leader. This breadth input is two sessions stale and is used only as structural context, not as Friday momentum evidence.

Strongest identifiable industries

Memory / data storage and semiconductor equipment were the clearest Friday winners. Sandisk rose roughly 12%, with additional strength in memory, storage and chip-equipment names.

Lagging Sectors and Subsectors

1. Consumer Discretionary — Friday sector laggard

Consumer discretionary was the weakest of the 11 S&P sectors. Lululemon fell 17.4% after cutting its annual forecasts.

The latest complete sector-breadth reading was also weak: on September 2 only about 28% of discretionary stocks were above the 20-day, 38% above the 50-day and 45% above the 200-day.

Status: Both short-term and structural weakness.

2. Software and Services — sharp relative reversal

Software and services fell 2.1% Friday even though the group remains up roughly 24% for the quarter. Adobe dropped 6.7% following a CEO transition.

Status: Friday laggard inside a stronger intermediate trend; not yet a durable structural laggard.

3. Real Estate — rate-sensitive structural laggard

The latest complete breadth data, from September 2, showed only about 23% above the 20-day and 20% above the 50-day, with 57% above the 200-day.

Status: Weak intermediate structure. Friday's increase in Treasury yields reinforces the existing rate-sensitive headwind, although a reliable final sector return was not available.

Event-driven weakness

Credit-reporting firms fell sharply after an FHFA directive affecting mortgage-credit scoring. Fair Isaac lost 16.7%, TransUnion 5.9%, and Equifax 6.4%. This is industry-specific rather than evidence of broad financial-sector deterioration.

Breadth and Internals

Friday's final exchange breadth was mixed:

  • NYSE: decliners led advancers 1.04-to-1
  • Nasdaq: 2,478 advancers vs. 2,256 decliners, or 1.10-to-1 positive
  • NYSE new highs/lows: 151 / 167
  • S&P 500 new highs/lows: 3 / 6
  • Nasdaq new highs/lows: 61 / 99
  • U.S. exchange volume: 13.14 billion shares, well below the 20-day average of 14.89 billion

The advance-decline data therefore did not confirm a broad selloff. The weaker signal remains the high/low structure: new lows exceeded highs across all three tracked universes.

Broad U.S. moving-average participation across NYSE/Nasdaq/AMEX improved to:

  • 44.6% above the 20-day
  • 49.7% above the 50-day
  • 57.5% above the 200-day

That compares with approximately 39.2% / 48.1% / 56.4% the prior session in the same dataset. This is a meaningful breadth repair underneath a down index day, although 50-day participation remains below 50%.

A current, index-specific September 4 S&P/Nasdaq/Russell moving-average table was not available. September 2 index-specific readings are therefore not presented as if they were Friday data.

RSP, the S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF, was down roughly 0.25%, versus the cap-weighted S&P's 0.38% decline. Equal weight therefore modestly outperformed rather than confirming renewed concentration.

Breadth grade: Mixed daily breadth / negative high-low breadth / improving intermediate participation / primary structure still intact.

Cross-Market Confirmation

Volatility

The VIX closed near 14.2–14.3, remaining near the low end of its 2026 range.

Assessment: Positive / non-confirming of stress. Volatility is inconsistent with a Defensive or Risk-Off classification.

Credit

The freshest verified broad U.S. high-yield OAS was approximately 2.66% on September 2, versus 2.65% the previous session. That remains historically tight despite a slight uptick.

Assessment: Slightly stale but still positive. Credit continues to be the strongest cross-market argument against a Defensive classification.

Treasury rates

The strong payroll report pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to roughly 4.78% and the 2-year toward 4.37%–4.41% as investors increased expectations for a September rate hike.

Assessment: Material negative confirmation. Rates remain the principal macro constraint on the equity regime.

U.S. dollar

The dollar strengthened after payrolls, with the dollar index moving above 99.9 intraday.

Assessment: Mild-to-moderate tightening signal.

Oil

Brent settled at $92.68 and WTI at $91.48. For the week, Brent rose 7.6% and WTI nearly 10% amid renewed U.S.-Iran military exchanges and impaired Middle East supply routes.

Assessment: Material macro contradiction. Even without another large Friday jump, elevated oil and record U.S. diesel prices keep the inflation channel active.

What Changed

  1. Thursday's clean risk-on synchronization did not persist. Stocks slipped while Treasury yields and Fed-hike probabilities rose after the strong payroll report.
  2. The breadth setback was milder than the index decline implies. Nasdaq advance-decline breadth remained positive and broad moving-average participation improved.
  3. High/low breadth remains the unresolved warning. New lows exceeded new highs on the NYSE, S&P 500 and Nasdaq.
  4. Semiconductor leadership strengthened sharply. SOX gained 3.4% despite broader index weakness.
  5. Macro pressure re-tightened. Strong jobs data lifted yields, the dollar and September hike expectations, while oil finished a sharply higher week.

Risks and Contradictions

Evidence supporting Neutral / stabilization: the S&P remains near record territory; Nasdaq A/D breadth was positive Friday; broad moving-average breadth improved; equal weight slightly outperformed cap weight; semiconductors rallied; VIX remains near 14; and credit spreads remain tight.

Evidence preventing Risk-On: high/low breadth remains negative across the main universes; broad 50-day participation is still below 50%; the 10-year is back near 4.8%; Fed-hike odds increased materially; the dollar strengthened; and oil/diesel remain inflationary.

Evidence against Defensive: Friday did not produce broad selling pressure, volatility did not rise, credit did not widen materially, and intermediate breadth actually improved.

The central contradiction is:

The internal market is repairing slowly even as the macro rate/inflation backdrop remains restrictive.

Educational Summary

Friday should be read as a test of the stabilization, not a reversal back to the deterioration seen Tuesday.

The four-session sequence is now:

Tuesday: broad deterioration + 2.8-to-1 negative breadth + oil/rates stress
Wednesday: strong breadth reversal + Russell leadership + VIX lower
Thursday: second positive breadth day + large-cap breakout + yields lower
Friday: indexes modestly lower + yields higher, but breadth mixed rather than broadly negative + semiconductors strong + VIX still near 14

That sequence leaves the market in a transition regime.

The primary trend remains positive. Short-term stress has receded substantially from Tuesday. Intermediate participation is improving, but not yet strong enough to support a Risk-On upgrade. At the same time, the rates/oil complex remains sufficiently restrictive that a clean bullish reacceleration has not been confirmed.

Current conclusion

NEUTRAL — Moderate-High confidence — Mixed stabilization

The highest-information point Friday is that a meaningful rates shock did not recreate the breadth damage seen earlier in the week. That is constructive. The counterpoint is that new-low pressure remains elevated and macro tightening has returned.

The next decisive evidence would normally come from whether 50-day participation can establish itself above 50%, Nasdaq new lows contract below new highs, and credit/volatility remain calm despite yields near 4.8%. A failure of those breadth measures accompanied by renewed volatility or credit widening would shift the balance back toward Defensive.
