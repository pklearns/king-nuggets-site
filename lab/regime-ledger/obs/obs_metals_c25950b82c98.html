Wednesday produced the first convincing repair attempt since the Jackson Hole break. Spot gold rebounded more than 1% to roughly $4,376, December futures settled near $4,414.60, and silver gained about 1.2%. Miners strongly outperformed: GDXJ finished near $128.05, +4.7%, while SIL was up roughly 4%+. Treasury yields and the dollar eased from intraday highs after weak private-payroll data. The correction remains in force, but deterioration stopped today.

What Changed

August ADP private payroll growth was only about 38,000. The 10-year Treasury yield retreated to roughly 4.78% after trading around 4.82%, while the dollar also eased after reaching a near three-week high. Gold and silver responded positively, but the stronger signal was miner breadth: Reuters identified gold and silver miners among Wednesday's equity leaders; GDXJ rose roughly 4.7% and SIL roughly 4%+.

Fed expectations remain restrictive. Markets still price roughly a 64% probability of a September hike, so Friday's employment report is the next major regime test.

Macro / Flow / Positioning Evidence

Rates/Fed: The latest available 10-year TIPS real yield remains around 2.4%, still restrictive. The monetary component is the largest tactical negative.

Dollar: DXY briefly reached roughly 99.85 before easing. The currency regime is no longer supportive as it was during the August breakout, but today's reversal prevents another downgrade.

Flows: Gold and other precious-metals funds attracted about $4.21 billion in the week through August 26, the strongest weekly inflow in six months. Sponsorship remains constructive, though the timing near the late-August peak argues against treating one week as conclusive.

Central banks: World Gold Council data show 289 tonnes of Q2 net buying. H1 demand of 345 tonnes was the weakest first half since 2022, but the 2026 survey remains structurally supportive: 89% of respondents expect global official gold reserves to rise over the next year and 45% expect their own holdings to increase.

CFTC: Latest positions are through August 25. Gold non-commercial traders held 277,159 longs vs 33,825 shorts, about +243,334 net, after longs rose by more than 20,000 contracts in one week. Silver was much less crowded at 37,871 longs vs 12,610 shorts, about +25,261 net, while open interest fell more than 6,000 contracts.

Physical: Gold jewellery demand remains price-sensitive. Silver retains the cleaner scarcity backdrop: the Silver Institute / Metals Focus outlook still points to a sixth consecutive structural deficit, around 46 million ounces, although PV thrifting/substitution is reducing industrial use.

Fiscal/Treasury: Treasury expects $739 billion of Q3 privately held net marketable borrowing and $628 billion in Q4. Fiscal pressure remains secularly supportive for reserve diversification but tactically adverse when it pushes term premium and real yields higher.

Trend & Confirmation

Trending move: August's bull move remains visible, but the active regime is still a correction of that move. Today's rebound is repair, not yet a resumed trend.

Silver: Improved, but it has not reclaimed the late-August $68–70 leadership area.

Gold miners: Strong daily confirmation. GDXJ materially outpaced bullion.

Silver miners: Strong daily confirmation. SIL reversed part of Tuesday's underperformance.

Failed breakout risk: Still active. The rebound needs follow-through.

Excess & Distribution Monitor

Excess Risk: 27/100 — low.
January 2026 similarity: 25/100.

The near-term risk is correction / failed breakout, not blow-off excess. Gold futures crowding remains the main January-like feature. Missing are repeated 4%–7% silver upside days, persistent miner divergence at fresh highs, extreme silver CFTC positioning, repeated failed highs near January records, and January-scale trading activity. WGC data show January turnover averaged roughly $623 billion/day, rising to about $963 billion/day in the final week, with gold ETF trading near $23 billion/day.

A future move toward $4,800–5,000 gold / $70–75 silver should trigger staged de-risking only if accompanied by COT expansion, miner divergence, extreme ETF inflows, widening ranges and failed highs.

Alternative Opportunity

No clearly superior liquid-market opportunity. Energy has momentum but severe geopolitical gap risk. Equities also remain exposed to payroll/Fed repricing, while long-duration Treasuries retain fiscal and inflation risk.

Action Implied

Maintain strategic exposure; do not add aggressively yet. The correction is stabilizing, but it is not repaired.

  • Gold holds $4,300–4,350 and miners retain relative strength: constructive correction/base.
  • Gold reclaims $4,525–4,550 and silver $67–68 with miner leadership: upgrade toward Bull Trend.
  • Gold loses $4,200–4,250 and silver $60–62 with miners leading lower: likely failed August breakout; raise bear probability materially.
  • A later move toward $4,800–5,000 / $70–75 with crowding and miner divergence: reactivate Bull Excess / Distribution Risk monitoring.

Bottom line: September 2 stopped the deterioration. Lower yields, a softer dollar off the highs and strong miner leadership produced the first high-quality repair day of the correction. Fed hike odds remain high, real yields remain restrictive and gold futures positioning is crowded. Friday's payroll report is the next major test.
