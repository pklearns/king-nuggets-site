What Changed

Gold fell to its lowest level in more than a month. Reuters reported spot gold at $4,312.59/oz and U.S. gold futures at $4,351.90/oz. Silver fell 1.2% in Reuters' report; the Wall Street Journal reported silver futures settling at $63.513/oz, down 1.6%.

Sources: Reuters, 2026-09-14; Wall Street Journal, 2026-09-14.

The dollar strengthened. Reuters reported the dollar index at 99.41, up 0.3%.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

The 10-year nominal Treasury yield breached 5% for the first time since 2023 and reached 5.01%. Reuters attributed the move to inflation concerns, oil, heavy debt issuance, and fiscal concerns.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

Fed tightening became close to fully priced. Reuters reports during the session put the probability of a September hike around 90%–93%.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

Macro / Flow / Positioning Evidence

Monetary policy and real yields

Scored status: UNAVAILABLE.

Current Fed expectations are clearly adverse for metals: Reuters reported roughly 90%–93% odds of a rate increase at the September 15–16 meeting.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

However, methodology v0.3 defines this as a combined monetary-policy and real-yield component. A same-session September 14 official Treasury real-yield observation was not retrievable at report time. No prior-session real yield is substituted.

Nominal yields provide unscored context: Reuters reported the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.01%.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

U.S. dollar

The dollar became a clear headwind. Reuters reported the dollar index rising 0.3% to 99.41, supported by Middle East stress, higher oil prices, and expectations of Fed tightening.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

ETF and fund flows

The World Gold Council's latest monthly report showed global physically backed gold ETFs attracted $18 billion in August, the second-largest monthly inflow on record. Holdings increased 121 tonnes to a record 4,189 tonnes.

Source: World Gold Council, 2026-09-09.

The latest weekly precious-metals fund-flow evidence available before this session had turned negative, preventing the component from receiving the near-maximum score implied by August alone.

Source: Reuters/LSEG Lipper, 2026-09-11.

Central-bank and reserve demand

Official-sector gold demand remains structurally supportive. World Gold Council data showed continued central-bank net buying in the latest available monthly data, while China reported another reserve increase for August.

Sources: World Gold Council, September 2026; People's Bank of China / SAFE, September 2026.

This support is gold-specific. It does not justify assigning silver an equivalent reserve-demand score.

CFTC positioning

The latest CFTC Commitments of Traders report covers positions as of September 8, 2026.

Gold non-commercial positions were 261,007 long and 29,047 short, a net long of 231,960 contracts. Longs increased by 522 from September 1 while shorts fell by 3,314.

Silver non-commercial positions were 36,245 long and 10,196 short, a net long of 26,049 contracts. Longs rose 842 while shorts rose 1,532 from September 1.

Source: CFTC Commitments of Traders, positions as of 2026-09-08.

Gold remains substantially more crowded than silver. That raises distribution sensitivity but does not by itself establish a bull-excess regime.

Physical supply and demand

Gold's physical picture remains balanced: strong official and investment demand coexist with healthy mine supply.

Source: World Gold Council Gold Demand Trends Q2 2026.

Silver retains stronger structural scarcity. The Silver Institute's 2026 outlook forecasts a sixth consecutive annual market deficit, approximately 46.3 million ounces, with mine production broadly flat.

Source: Silver Institute World Silver Survey 2026.

Fiscal and Treasury financing

The Treasury market is increasingly part of the metals story. Reuters reported the 10-year yield reaching 5.01%, citing heavy issuance and growing concern over deficits and national debt alongside inflation.

Source: Reuters, 2026-09-14.

This remains a two-sided force. Fiscal credibility and debt-supply concerns strengthen gold's strategic case, but the immediate transmission through higher nominal and likely real yields is tactically adverse.

Long-duration Treasuries now offer materially higher nominal yields, but buying duration into an active bond selloff is not obviously superior risk/reward. Oil has strong geopolitical momentum, but Brent settled at $105.68/bbl after approaching $110 intraday, leaving substantial headline and gap risk.

Source: Associated Press, 2026-09-14.

Cash and short-duration government securities remain comparatively attractive defensively, but there is insufficient evidence to elevate them into a separate tactical opportunity call.

Action Implied

The independent evidence supports correction, with the tactical picture weaker than Friday.

The central issue is no longer whether the August metals advance was strong; it was. The issue is whether structural demand can overcome a renewed tightening impulse. On September 14 the answer remained no: gold and silver fell, the dollar strengthened, the 10-year Treasury yield breached 5%, and a Fed hike became close to fully priced.

The secular case has not failed. Gold ETF holdings remain at record levels, central banks continue to accumulate reserves, fiscal-financing concerns are increasing, and silver remains structurally undersupplied.

A return to bull-trend requires evidence of durable price recovery plus relief in the rates/dollar impulse and verifiable miner confirmation. Until those conditions emerge, staged additions based solely on the secular thesis have inferior tactical evidence to waiting for confirmation.
