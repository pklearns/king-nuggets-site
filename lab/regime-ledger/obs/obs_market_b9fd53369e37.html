Daily Market Regime Watch

Latest completed U.S. session: Friday, August 28, 2026

Regime Snapshot

Regime: Neutral
Confidence: Moderate-High
Direction of change: Short-term deterioration; primary trend intact

The S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,711.76, the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.52% to 26,402.42, and the Russell 2000 fell 1.4% to 2,972.37. For the week, however, the S&P gained 0.49% and Nasdaq 0.85%, while Russell lost 1.5%. The market therefore remains close to its highs, but Friday exposed weaker participation beneath the large-cap indexes. (Sources: Reuters; AP.)

The regime stays Neutral rather than Defensive because long-term participation remains healthy, credit spreads are still tight, volatility is subdued, and the primary price trends have not broken. It does not return to Risk-On because Friday produced roughly 2-to-1 negative Nasdaq breadth, Nasdaq new lows exceeded new highs by more than 2-to-1, Russell materially underperformed, and only about half of S&P, Nasdaq and Russell constituents remain above their 50-day averages. (Sources: Reuters; Barchart; ICE BofA/FRED.)

Index Scorecard

S&P 500 Positive Weakening / consolidating Stalled below highs Mixed 42% above 20-day, 53% above 50-day, 69% above 200-day
Nasdaq Composite Positive Fragile Reversal after Thursday rally Weakening 41% above 20-day, 49% above 50-day, 64% above 200-day; new lows > highs
Russell 2000 Positive Weakening Breakdown below 3,000 Weak Durable YTD leader, but 38% above 20-day and 49% above 50-day

S&P 500: The index remains within roughly 1% of its record and finished the week higher. The primary trend is therefore still constructive. Internally, however, only 53% of S&P constituents are above their 50-day averages and 42% are above their 20-day averages, versus 69% above the 200-day. That is a classic split between healthy long-term structure and weaker intermediate participation. (Source: Barchart.)

Nasdaq Composite: Nasdaq gave back part of Thursday's Nvidia-led surge. Friday's breadth was materially weaker than the index decline: 3,272 decliners versus 1,494 advancers, while 120 new lows exceeded 51 new highs. Nasdaq participation is also below 50% above the 50-day average. This remains the clearest index-level warning. (Sources: Reuters; Barchart.)

Russell 2000: Russell fell 1.4% Friday and 1.5% for the week, closing below 3,000. It still leads the major indexes year to date at roughly +19.8%, so the durable relative-strength signal has not disappeared. The short-term signal has: only 49% of Russell constituents are above their 50-day averages and 38% above their 20-day averages. (Sources: AP; Barchart.)

Leadership and Rotation

The market currently has a split leadership structure.

Durable capitalization leadership: small caps still lead year to date, but that advantage is eroding on the short horizon. Russell's Friday and weekly underperformance means small caps are no longer confirming the large-cap advance.

Factor leadership: current breadth modestly favors value over growth. About 56% of S&P 500 Value constituents are above their 50-day averages versus 46% for S&P 500 Growth; the 200-day readings are similar at 70% and 69%. This is not a dramatic value rotation, but it is broader intermediate participation than growth. (Source: Barchart.)

Growth leadership: AI and semiconductors remain powerful long-term themes but are increasingly two-sided. Thursday's Nvidia-driven technology surge was followed Friday by Nvidia -4.6% and Marvell -10.3%. That makes technology a source of index volatility rather than clean regime confirmation. (Source: Reuters.)

Leading Sectors and Subsectors

The ranking below emphasizes trend breadth plus recent relative strength, not Friday's one-day return alone.

1. Energy — durable breadth leader

Energy has the strongest moving-average participation among S&P sectors: approximately 71% above the 20-day, 90% above the 50-day, and 86% above the 200-day. Oil eased Friday, so the sector did not receive a fresh commodity tailwind, but its underlying breadth remains the strongest. (Source: Barchart.)

2. Financials — strong intermediate and primary participation

Financials show about 53% above the 20-day, 66% above the 50-day, and 88% above the 200-day. This remains one of the cleaner combinations of intermediate and long-term participation. (Source: Barchart.)

3. Communication Services — strongest short-term participation

Communication services show approximately 68% of constituents above the 20-, 50- and 5-day averages and were the largest positive sector contribution Friday, supported by Alphabet's 1.7% gain. Its 200-day participation is a more modest 59%, so this is stronger short-term than long-term leadership. (Sources: Reuters; Barchart.)

Subsectors / industries

Semiconductors and AI chips were the clearest weak subsector Friday, with Nvidia down 4.6% and Marvell down 10.3%. No reliable end-of-day dataset reviewed supports a full industry-group ranking beyond that observation, so no broader subsector league table is forced. (Source: Reuters.)

Lagging Sectors and Subsectors

1. Utilities — weakest breadth structure

Only about 10% of S&P utilities are above their 20- and 50-day averages and 26% above the 200-day. That is the weakest trend breadth of the 11 S&P sectors. (Source: Barchart.)

2. Consumer Discretionary — weak across horizons

Consumer discretionary has roughly 23% above the 20-day, 40% above the 50-day, and 47% above the 200-day. It is one of the few sectors with fewer than half its members above the long-term average. (Source: Barchart.)

3. Real Estate — weak intermediate participation

Real estate has only about 31% above the 20-day and 24% above the 50-day, despite 69% remaining above the 200-day. That profile suggests substantial intermediate deterioration inside a still-positive primary structure. (Source: Barchart.)

Technology was a major Friday laggard because of chips, but its 77% above-200-day reading remains stronger than the structural laggards above. It is therefore better described as unstable former leadership than a bottom-ranked sector. (Sources: Reuters; Barchart.)

Breadth and Internals

Friday's closing breadth was negative:

  • NYSE: decliners led advancers 1.77-to-1
  • Nasdaq: 1,494 advancers vs. 3,272 decliners, or 2.19-to-1 negative
  • NYSE new highs/lows: 167 / 107
  • S&P 500 new highs/lows: 5 / 3
  • Nasdaq new highs/lows: 51 / 120
  • Volume: 15.68 billion shares, close to but slightly below the 20-day average of 15.8 billion (Source: Reuters.)

Moving-average breadth confirms an intermediate soft patch rather than a primary breakdown:

  • S&P 500: 42% above 20-day, 53% above 50-day, 69% above 200-day
  • Nasdaq Composite: 41% / 49% / 64%
  • Russell 2000: 38% / 49% / 63% (Source: Barchart.)

The equal-weight S&P 500's final August 28 return was not reliably available in the sources reviewed. It remains unscored rather than estimated. Thursday's cap-weighted advance had already been notably narrow, so Friday's negative exchange breadth reinforces the concentration concern even without a verified RSP close.

Breadth grade: Negative short term / Neutral intermediate / Positive long term.

Cross-Market Confirmation

Volatility

The VIX rose only modestly to about 14.63 after trading as low as 14.13 intraday, its lowest since December 30. It still fell roughly 3.6% for the week. (Source: MarketWatch.)

Assessment: Positive for the primary regime, but a contradiction to weak breadth. The options market is not pricing broad stress.

Credit

As of August 27, broad U.S. high-yield OAS was about 270 bps and investment-grade OAS 81 bps. High-yield spreads widened only 1 bp on the day and remained 5 bps tighter week over week. (Source: ICE BofA/FRED-derived data.)

Assessment: Positive confirmation. Credit is not validating a Defensive or Risk-Off equity regime.

Treasury rates / Fed expectations

The 2-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.31% and the 10-year to roughly 4.69% after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized the 2% inflation target and the need for further progress on inflation. Market-implied odds of a September rate increase rose from roughly 35% to 56%. (Source: Reuters.)

Assessment: Negative. The front-end repricing is especially relevant for high-duration growth and small caps.

U.S. dollar

The dollar index rose about 0.36% to 99.47. (Source: Reuters.)

Assessment: Mild negative confirmation through tighter financial conditions.

Oil

WTI slipped about 0.6% to $83.00, while Brent eased roughly 0.3% to $89.40. (Source: Reuters.)

Assessment: Mildly supportive. Oil is no longer intensifying the tightening signal, although absolute prices remain elevated.

What Changed

  1. Thursday's AI-led breakout failed to broaden. Nvidia reversed sharply Friday and Nasdaq breadth deteriorated to more than 2-to-1 negative. (Source: Reuters.)
  2. Russell weakened materially. Small caps fell 1.4% Friday and 1.5% for the week, while fewer than half of Russell constituents remain above the 50-day average. (Sources: AP; Barchart.)
  3. Nasdaq new-low pressure expanded. New lows outnumbered new highs 120 to 51, the strongest internal warning in today's data. (Source: Reuters.)
  4. Fed expectations tightened. Warsh's speech pushed September hike probabilities materially higher and lifted the 2-year yield and dollar. (Source: Reuters.)
  5. Long-term confirmation remains intact. Roughly 69% of S&P stocks remain above their 200-day averages, VIX is below 15, and high-yield spreads remain near historically tight levels. (Sources: Barchart; MarketWatch; ICE BofA/FRED-derived data.)

Risks and Contradictions

Evidence against a Defensive downgrade: the S&P and Nasdaq finished the week higher; primary trends remain near record territory; 69% of S&P constituents remain above the 200-day; VIX is around 14.6; and credit spreads remain tight.

Evidence against a Risk-On upgrade: Friday breadth was materially negative; Nasdaq new lows more than doubled new highs; Russell broke below 3,000 and lost ground for the week; intermediate moving-average participation has fallen toward 50%; and Fed tightening expectations rose sharply.

The central contradiction is:

Price, volatility and credit still describe a healthy primary bull structure, while breadth, small caps and short-term growth leadership describe a weakening intermediate environment.

Educational Summary

Friday strengthens the case for Neutral as the correct transition regime.

Thursday demonstrated that strong AI earnings can still lift the cap-weighted indexes rapidly. Friday demonstrated the limitation: that strength did not broaden, Russell weakened, Nasdaq new lows expanded, and a more hawkish Fed repricing pressured rate-sensitive assets. At the same time, there is no evidence of systemic risk aversion: VIX remains unusually low, credit remains tight, and most S&P stocks remain above their 200-day averages.

The evidence therefore separates cleanly by horizon:

Primary: positive.
Intermediate: mixed-to-weakening.
Short term: deteriorating.

Current conclusion

NEUTRAL — Moderate-High confidence — Short-term deterioration; primary trend intact

A return to Risk-On would normally require breadth to recover above the 50-day horizon, Nasdaq new lows to contract, and Russell/equal-weight participation to rejoin the large-cap indexes. A move toward Defensive would require the opposite deterioration to spread into the primary structure—especially sustained new-low expansion, rising volatility, widening credit spreads, and a larger share of stocks losing their 200-day trends.

**Durable capitalization leadership:** small caps still lead year to date, but that advantage is eroding on the short horizon. Russell's Friday and weekly underperformance means small caps are no longer confirming the large-cap advance.

**Factor leadership:** current breadth modestly favors value over growth. About **56% of S&P 500 Value** constituents are above their 50-day averages versus **46% for S&P 500 Growth**; the 200-day readings are similar at 70% and 69%. This is not a dramatic value rotation, but it is broader intermediate participation than growth. (Source: Barchart.)

**Growth leadership:** AI and semiconductors remain powerful long-term themes but are increasingly two-sided. Thursday's Nvidia-driven technology surge was followed Friday by Nvidia **-4.6%** and Marvell **-10.3%**. That makes technology a source of index volatility rather than clean regime confirmation. (Source: Reuters.)

## Leading Sectors and Subsectors

The ranking below emphasizes **trend breadth plus recent relative strength**, not Friday's one-day return alone.

### 1. Energy — durable breadth leader

Energy has the strongest moving-average participation among S&P sectors: approximately **71% above the 20-day**, **90% above the 50-day**, and **86% above the 200-day**. Oil eased Friday, so the sector did not receive a fresh commodity tailwind, but its underlying breadth remains the strongest. (Source: Barchart.)

### 2. Financials — strong intermediate and primary participation

Financials show about **53% above the 20-day**, **66% above the 50-day**, and **88% above the 200-day**. This remains one of the cleaner combinations of intermediate and long-term participation. (Source: Barchart.)

### 3. Communication Services — strongest short-term participation

Communication services show approximately **68% of constituents above the 20-, 50- and 5-day averages** and were the largest positive sector contribution Friday, supported by Alphabet's **1.7%** gain. Its 200-day participation is a more modest 59%, so this is stronger short-term than long-term leadership. (Sources: Reuters; Barchart.)

### Subsectors / industries

**Semiconductors and AI chips** were the clearest weak subsector Friday, with Nvidia down 4.6% and Marvell down 10.3%. No reliable end-of-day dataset reviewed supports a full industry-group ranking beyond that observation, so no broader subsector league table is forced. (Source: Reuters.)

## Lagging Sectors and Subsectors

### 1. Utilities — weakest breadth structure

Only about **10%** of S&P utilities are above their 20- and 50-day averages and **26%** above the 200-day. That is the weakest trend breadth of the 11 S&P sectors. (Source: Barchart.)

### 2. Consumer Discretionary — weak across horizons

Consumer discretionary has roughly **23% above the 20-day**, **40% above the 50-day**, and **47% above the 200-day**. It is one of the few sectors with fewer than half its members above the long-term average. (Source: Barchart.)

### 3. Real Estate — weak intermediate participation

Real estate has only about **31% above the 20-day** and **24% above the 50-day**, despite 69% remaining above the 200-day. That profile suggests substantial intermediate deterioration inside a still-positive primary structure. (Source: Barchart.)

**Technology** was a major Friday laggard because of chips, but its **77% above-200-day** reading remains stronger than the structural laggards above. It is therefore better described as unstable former leadership than a bottom-ranked sector. (Sources: Reuters; Barchart.)

## Cross-Market Confirmation

### Volatility

The VIX rose only modestly to about **14.63** after trading as low as 14.13 intraday, its lowest since December 30. It still fell roughly 3.6% for the week. (Source: MarketWatch.)

**Assessment:** Positive for the primary regime, but a contradiction to weak breadth. The options market is not pricing broad stress.

### Credit

As of August 27, broad U.S. high-yield OAS was about **270 bps** and investment-grade OAS **81 bps**. High-yield spreads widened only 1 bp on the day and remained 5 bps tighter week over week. (Source: ICE BofA/FRED-derived data.)

**Assessment:** Positive confirmation. Credit is not validating a Defensive or Risk-Off equity regime.

### Treasury rates / Fed expectations

The **2-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.31%** and the **10-year to roughly 4.69%** after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized the 2% inflation target and the need for further progress on inflation. Market-implied odds of a September rate increase rose from roughly **35% to 56%**. (Source: Reuters.)

**Assessment:** Negative. The front-end repricing is especially relevant for high-duration growth and small caps.

### U.S. dollar

The dollar index rose about **0.36% to 99.47**. (Source: Reuters.)

**Assessment:** Mild negative confirmation through tighter financial conditions.

### Oil

WTI slipped about **0.6% to $83.00**, while Brent eased roughly **0.3% to $89.40**. (Source: Reuters.)

**Assessment:** Mildly supportive. Oil is no longer intensifying the tightening signal, although absolute prices remain elevated.

## What Changed

1. **Thursday's AI-led breakout failed to broaden.** Nvidia reversed sharply Friday and Nasdaq breadth deteriorated to more than 2-to-1 negative. (Source: Reuters.)
2. **Russell weakened materially.** Small caps fell 1.4% Friday and 1.5% for the week, while fewer than half of Russell constituents remain above the 50-day average. (Sources: AP; Barchart.)
3. **Nasdaq new-low pressure expanded.** New lows outnumbered new highs **120 to 51**, the strongest internal warning in today's data. (Source: Reuters.)
4. **Fed expectations tightened.** Warsh's speech pushed September hike probabilities materially higher and lifted the 2-year yield and dollar. (Source: Reuters.)
5. **Long-term confirmation remains intact.** Roughly 69% of S&P stocks remain above their 200-day averages, VIX is below 15, and high-yield spreads remain near historically tight levels. (Sources: Barchart; MarketWatch; ICE BofA/FRED-derived data.)

## Risks and Contradictions

**Evidence against a Defensive downgrade:** the S&P and Nasdaq finished the week higher; primary trends remain near record territory; 69% of S&P constituents remain above the 200-day; VIX is around 14.6; and credit spreads remain tight.

**Evidence against a Risk-On upgrade:** Friday breadth was materially negative; Nasdaq new lows more than doubled new highs; Russell broke below 3,000 and lost ground for the week; intermediate moving-average participation has fallen toward 50%; and Fed tightening expectations rose sharply.

The central contradiction is:

> **Price, volatility and credit still describe a healthy primary bull structure, while breadth, small caps and short-term growth leadership describe a weakening intermediate environment.**

## Educational Summary

Friday strengthens the case for **Neutral** as the correct transition regime.

Thursday demonstrated that strong AI earnings can still lift the cap-weighted indexes rapidly. Friday demonstrated the limitation: that strength did not broaden, Russell weakened, Nasdaq new lows expanded, and a more hawkish Fed repricing pressured rate-sensitive assets. At the same time, there is no evidence of systemic risk aversion: VIX remains unusually low, credit remains tight, and most S&P stocks remain above their 200-day averages.

The evidence therefore separates cleanly by horizon:

**Primary:** positive.  
**Intermediate:** mixed-to-weakening.  
**Short term:** deteriorating.

### Current conclusion

**NEUTRAL — Moderate-High confidence — Short-term deterioration; primary trend intact**

A return to Risk-On would normally require breadth to recover above the 50-day horizon, Nasdaq new lows to contract, and Russell/equal-weight participation to rejoin the large-cap indexes. A move toward Defensive would require the opposite deterioration to spread into the primary structure—especially sustained new-low expansion, rising volatility, widening credit spreads, and a larger share of stocks losing their 200-day trends.
