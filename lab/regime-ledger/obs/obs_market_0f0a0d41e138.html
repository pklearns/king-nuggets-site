Daily Market Regime Watch

Latest completed U.S. session: Tuesday, August 25, 2026

Regime Snapshot

Regime: Neutral
Confidence: Moderate-High
Direction of change: Improving, but not yet confirmed

The S&P 500 rose 0.32% to 7,677.24, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.66% to 26,151.30, and the Dow added 0.30% to 53,577.40. The Russell 2000 gained about 0.5%. The rebound reversed part of Monday’s technology-led weakness, while falling oil and Treasury yields eased two of the principal macro pressures that pushed the regime into Neutral.

The improvement was broader than the headline indexes: NYSE advancers beat decliners 1.71-to-1 and Nasdaq advancers 1.76-to-1; new highs exceeded new lows on both exchanges. However, the cap-weighted S&P still materially outperformed equal weight, with RSP down about 0.16%, and the strongest sector leadership came from technology ahead of Nvidia earnings. That combination supports improvement, but not a return to Risk-On yet.

Index Scorecard

Nasdaq Composite Positive Repairing Strong rebound Improved Session leader; still dependent on AI/chips

S&P 500: Tuesday recovered Monday’s loss and returned the index to roughly 1.6% below its August 13 record. The primary trend remains positive; the intermediate trend is still best described as consolidation/repair because recent breadth and leadership have been unstable.

Nasdaq: The Nasdaq led Tuesday as semiconductors rebounded. The PHLX Semiconductor Index rose about 1.4% after falling roughly 2.7% Monday. Nvidia gained 2.2%, Micron 2.5%, and AMD 4.9%. This improves short-term momentum but does not yet repair the prior week’s semiconductor weakness.

Russell 2000: Small caps gained about 0.5%, restoring some confirmation after Monday’s decline. Russell remains the strongest major U.S. index year to date, but the recent intermediate pullback keeps its regime below fully confirmed Risk-On.

Leadership and Rotation

Tuesday favored large-cap growth, semiconductors and selected biotechnology, while broad equal-weight performance lagged. This was therefore a better tape than Monday, but not a clean broadening day.

The durable leadership picture remains mixed: Russell 2000 continues to lead year to date, while technology/semiconductors remain the principal short-term swing factor. Tuesday’s chip rebound should be treated as repair, not yet durable reacceleration, because Nvidia earnings on August 26 are a major event risk and the sector has recently shown sharp two-way volatility.

Leading Sectors and Subsectors

1. Information Technology

Technology was the clearest sector leader. Semiconductor strength drove much of the move, with SOX +1.4%, Nvidia +2.2%, Micron +2.5%, and AMD +4.9%.

Status: Short-term improving; intermediate leadership not fully repaired.

2. Communication Services

Communication services also finished among the strongest S&P sectors, supported by Meta and other mega-cap growth exposure.

Status: Relative strength, but still part of the concentrated large-cap growth complex.

3. Semiconductors / AI hardware

Semiconductors were the strongest identifiable subsector. The move was meaningful because it followed Monday’s sharp selloff, but it remains event-sensitive ahead of Nvidia results.

4. Biotechnology

Moderna rose about 14% after a target-price increase following encouraging cancer-vaccine trial results with Merck.

Status: Strong catalyst-driven leadership; not sufficient by itself to rank biotechnology as a durable market leader.

Lagging Sectors and Subsectors

Energy

Energy lagged as Brent and WTI fell more than 3%. Lower crude is negative for energy relative strength but positive for the broader inflation and rates backdrop.

Retail / consumer discretionary pockets

Dick’s Sporting Goods fell 30.7% after cutting annual forecasts. Target fell 3.8%, while Nike also weakened.

Interpretation: Consumer discretionary remains highly dispersed rather than uniformly weak, but Tuesday added evidence that parts of retail are under pressure as consumer confidence fell to a seven-month low.

Breadth and Internals

Tuesday’s final exchange breadth was constructive:

  • NYSE: advancers led decliners 1.71-to-1
  • Nasdaq: 3,059 advancers vs. 1,740 decliners, or 1.76-to-1
  • NYSE new highs/lows: 190 / 79
  • S&P 500 new highs/lows: 11 / 4
  • Nasdaq new highs/lows: 105 / 85

This is a clear improvement from Monday, when NYSE breadth was slightly negative and Nasdaq breadth was about 1.5-to-1 negative.

The contradiction is equal weight. The Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF (RSP) fell about 0.16% while the cap-weighted S&P rose 0.32%. That indicates that Tuesday’s positive exchange breadth did not translate into broad S&P 500 constituent strength; the index gain remained concentrated in larger growth names.

Reliable current readings for the percentage of S&P stocks above their 20-, 50- and 200-day moving averages were not available from a sufficiently current authoritative source.

Breadth grade: Positive exchange breadth / mixed large-cap breadth.

Cross-Market Confirmation

Volatility

A reliable official August 25 VIX closing print was not available from the sources reviewed. Monday’s VIX was 15.91, still low in absolute terms. Tuesday’s volatility input is therefore unscored rather than inferred.

Credit

Broad U.S. high-yield OAS was 2.69% on August 24, versus 2.70% on August 21 and 2.75% on August 20. Credit therefore remains orderly and is not confirming an equity stress regime. The latest Single-B reading directly verified from FRED is 2.94% on August 20.

Assessment: Neutral-to-positive confirmation.

Treasury rates

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.65% and the 30-year to about 5.18%, their second consecutive daily decline.

Assessment: Positive daily confirmation. The absolute levels remain restrictive, and part of the decline reflects Treasury buyback policy rather than a clean fundamental easing in inflation/fiscal risk.

Oil

Brent fell 3.9% to $88.58 and WTI 3.1% to $82.36, both at roughly one-week lows.

Assessment: Positive for the broad equity regime because it eases near-term inflation pressure; negative for energy relative strength.

U.S. dollar

The dollar index was roughly flat to slightly lower near 98.9.

Assessment: Neutral to mildly supportive.

What Changed

  1. Breadth reversed positively: both NYSE and Nasdaq moved from negative Monday breadth to roughly 1.7-to-1 positive Tuesday.
  2. Semiconductors rebounded: SOX gained about 1.4% after Monday’s 2.7% decline.
  3. Russell regained short-term confirmation: small caps rose about 0.5% alongside the large-cap indexes.
  4. Macro pressure eased materially: oil fell more than 3% and long Treasury yields declined for a second day.
  5. Concentration remained a warning: equal-weight S&P fell despite the cap-weighted index rising.

Risks and Contradictions

Confirming improvement: all major indexes advanced; Russell participated; exchange breadth was strongly positive; new highs exceeded new lows; semiconductors rebounded; oil and Treasury yields fell; high-yield credit remains orderly.

Contradicting evidence: RSP lagged and declined; technology was the primary driver of index gains; Nvidia earnings create unusually high event concentration; semiconductors remain volatile rather than clearly repaired; consumer confidence fell to a seven-month low; long rates remain high in absolute terms.

The central contradiction is:

Exchange-level participation and macro conditions improved, while S&P equal-weight participation still lagged and the rebound depended heavily on technology ahead of a major earnings catalyst.

Educational Summary

Tuesday improves the Neutral regime meaningfully but does not yet justify upgrading back to Risk-On.

The sequence is now:

Monday: tech/chips ↓ + Nasdaq/Russell ↓ + breadth weak
Tuesday: indexes ↑ + Russell ↑ + exchange breadth ↑↑ + chips ↑ + oil ↓↓ + yields ↓

That is the type of reversal normally associated with stabilization after an intermediate pullback. The reason to retain Neutral is that the repair is only one session old and remains concentrated in the same technology complex whose weakness drove the recent deterioration.

A durable Risk-On recovery would normally show continued positive advance-decline breadth, equal-weight confirmation, Russell persistence, semiconductor follow-through, contained new lows, and long yields remaining below their recent extremes.

A renewed deterioration would be more consequential if Tuesday’s breadth improvement fails immediately, equal weight continues to lag, semiconductor weakness resumes after Nvidia earnings, or credit begins widening alongside equities.

Tuesday repaired several of Monday’s negatives and materially reduced immediate Defensive risk. The regime remains Neutral because the intermediate leadership structure is still unstable and the latest rebound has not yet demonstrated persistence beyond a single session.
