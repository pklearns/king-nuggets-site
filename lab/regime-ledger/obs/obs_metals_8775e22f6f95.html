Tactical and Secular Probabilities

Tactical 1--6 months: bull 20% / base 40% / bear 40%.

The tactical balance deteriorated again. Real yields rose sharply for a second consecutive session, Fed-hike odds increased, the dollar remained near a two-month high, gold hit a one-week low and silver weakened more sharply. The principal tactical bull case is now a reversal in rates/dollar conditions rather than momentum continuation.

Secular 12--24 months: bull 57% / base 28% / bear 15%.

The secular case remains positive but has weakened at the margin because sustained real yields near 3% at the long end are a materially higher hurdle. Record ETF holdings, central-bank accumulation, fiscal financing pressure and silver's structural supply deficit remain important offsets.

What Changed

The most important development was another abrupt rise in real rates. Official Treasury data show the 10-year real yield at 2.77%, up from 2.70% Wednesday and 2.56% Tuesday. The 30-year real yield reached 3.08%, up from 2.99% Wednesday. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-24.

Nominal yields also accelerated. Reuters reported the 10-year Treasury yield reaching 5.20% and the 30-year reaching 5.48%, its highest since 2004. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Fed expectations hardened further: traders priced a 69% chance of an October rate hike, while Fed officials continued emphasizing inflation risks. Source: Reuters/CME FedWatch, 2026-09-24.

Gold consequently reached its lowest level since September 16. Spot silver fell 1.3% to $63.60/oz in Reuters' 2:00 p.m. ET observation. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Macro / Flow / Positioning Evidence

Monetary policy: The September 16 increase to a 3.75%-4.00% federal-funds target remains the current policy state. Reuters reported September 24 that the Fed signaled further hikes this year and Governor Michael Barr was the latest official to emphasize tighter policy. Sources: Federal Reserve, 2026-09-16; Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Real rates: Treasury's official September 24 real curve reads 2.70% at 5 years, 2.77% at 10 years, 2.85% at 20 years and 3.08% at 30 years. These are same-session official observations and represent a clear tightening impulse. Source: U.S. Treasury Daily Treasury Par Real Yield Curve Rates, 2026-09-24.

Rates expectations: CME FedWatch pricing reported by Reuters put an October hike at 69%. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Dollar: Reuters reported the dollar at a two-month high. No exact DXY level is used because a sufficiently authoritative same-session closing observation was not verified. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

ETF/fund flows: The latest valid monthly World Gold Council data remain strongly supportive: August physically backed gold ETFs attracted $18 billion, adding 121 tonnes and lifting holdings to a record 4,189 tonnes. Source: World Gold Council, September 2026, covering August.

Central banks: The latest valid periodic evidence remains supportive. Official-sector gold accumulation continued through August, including a substantial Chinese addition. Source: World Gold Council, September 2026.

Futures positioning: The latest scheduled CFTC report remains the September 18 release covering positions through September 15. Gold speculative positioning is positive and materially more crowded than silver, but neither observation by itself signals a January-style speculative climax. Source: CFTC Commitments of Traders, positions as of 2026-09-15.

Physical demand: High prices continue to constrain some price-sensitive gold demand. Silver retains its multi-year structural deficit, but the Silver Institute's 2026 outlook also forecasts weaker industrial demand as photovoltaic thrifting/substitution offsets strength in AI, autos and grids. Sources: World Gold Council, 2026; Silver Institute World Silver Survey 2026.

Fiscal/Treasury financing: The bond selloff is now itself a major macro catalyst. Reuters reported the 30-year Treasury yield reaching 5.48% and the 10-year 5.20%, with high energy costs, resilient growth and increased government spending cited as drivers. Reuters also reported that Treasury has expanded buybacks of 20- and 30-year debt as officials attempt to contain borrowing costs. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Alternative Opportunity

No clearly superior risk-reward opportunity is identified.

Cash and short-duration Treasury instruments have become more attractive as a defensive allocation because policy rates and market yields provide substantial carry without duration exposure. That is a liquidity/optionality choice rather than an asymmetric directional trade. Long-duration Treasuries remain vulnerable to inflation, supply and term-premium pressure; equities are increasingly exposed to the same discount-rate shock hurting metals.

Action Implied

Maintain correction and reduce tactical aggressiveness.

The secular evidence does not justify abandoning strategic metals exposure: ETF holdings and central-bank demand remain unusually strong, while fiscal financing pressure remains a long-run monetary-hedge argument. But those structural factors are currently losing the tactical contest against real yields, the dollar and Fed expectations.

New tactical exposure should require evidence of reversal rather than merely lower prices. The most persuasive repair would be a combination of falling real yields, a softer dollar, gold holding above the $4,235 support reference, and verified same-session silver/miner leadership.

A break below $4,235 with real yields still rising would materially increase the probability of a deeper correction toward the broader $4,000 area cited by Saxo Bank.

## Tactical and Secular Probabilities

**Tactical 1--6 months:** bull **20%** / base **40%** / bear **40%**.

The tactical balance deteriorated again. Real yields rose sharply for a
second consecutive session, Fed-hike odds increased, the dollar remained
near a two-month high, gold hit a one-week low and silver weakened more
sharply. The principal tactical bull case is now a reversal in
rates/dollar conditions rather than momentum continuation.

**Secular 12--24 months:** bull **57%** / base **28%** / bear **15%**.

The secular case remains positive but has weakened at the margin because
sustained real yields near 3% at the long end are a materially higher
hurdle. Record ETF holdings, central-bank accumulation, fiscal financing
pressure and silver's structural supply deficit remain important
offsets.

## What Changed

The most important development was another abrupt rise in real rates.
Official Treasury data show the **10-year real yield at 2.77%**, up from
**2.70%** Wednesday and **2.56%** Tuesday. The **30-year real yield
reached 3.08%**, up from 2.99% Wednesday. Source: U.S. Treasury,
2026-09-24.

Nominal yields also accelerated. Reuters reported the **10-year Treasury
yield reaching 5.20%** and the **30-year reaching 5.48%**, its highest
since 2004. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Fed expectations hardened further: traders priced a **69% chance of an
October rate hike**, while Fed officials continued emphasizing inflation
risks. Source: Reuters/CME FedWatch, 2026-09-24.

Gold consequently reached its lowest level since September 16. Spot
silver fell **1.3% to \$63.60/oz** in Reuters' 2:00 p.m. ET observation.
Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

## Alternative Opportunity

**No clearly superior risk-reward opportunity is identified.**

Cash and short-duration Treasury instruments have become more attractive
as a defensive allocation because policy rates and market yields provide
substantial carry without duration exposure. That is a
liquidity/optionality choice rather than an asymmetric directional
trade. Long-duration Treasuries remain vulnerable to inflation, supply
and term-premium pressure; equities are increasingly exposed to the same
discount-rate shock hurting metals.

## Action Implied

Maintain **correction** and reduce tactical aggressiveness.

The secular evidence does not justify abandoning strategic metals
exposure: ETF holdings and central-bank demand remain unusually strong,
while fiscal financing pressure remains a long-run monetary-hedge
argument. But those structural factors are currently losing the tactical
contest against real yields, the dollar and Fed expectations.

New tactical exposure should require evidence of reversal rather than
merely lower prices. The most persuasive repair would be a combination
of falling real yields, a softer dollar, gold holding above the
**\$4,235** support reference, and verified same-session silver/miner
leadership.

A break below **\$4,235** with real yields still rising would materially
increase the probability of a deeper correction toward the broader
**\$4,000** area cited by Saxo Bank.
