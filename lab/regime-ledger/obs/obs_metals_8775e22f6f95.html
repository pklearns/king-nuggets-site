Tactical and Secular Probabilities

Tactical 1--6 months: bull 20% / base 40% / bear 40%.

The tactical balance deteriorated again. Real yields rose sharply for a second consecutive session, Fed-hike odds increased, the dollar remained near a two-month high, gold hit a one-week low and silver weakened more sharply. The principal tactical bull case is now a reversal in rates/dollar conditions rather than momentum continuation.

Secular 12--24 months: bull 57% / base 28% / bear 15%.

The secular case remains positive but has weakened at the margin because sustained real yields near 3% at the long end are a materially higher hurdle. Record ETF holdings, central-bank accumulation, fiscal financing pressure and silver's structural supply deficit remain important offsets.

What Changed

The most important development was another abrupt rise in real rates. Official Treasury data show the 10-year real yield at 2.77%, up from 2.70% Wednesday and 2.56% Tuesday. The 30-year real yield reached 3.08%, up from 2.99% Wednesday. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-24.

Nominal yields also accelerated. Reuters reported the 10-year Treasury yield reaching 5.20% and the 30-year reaching 5.48%, its highest since 2004. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Fed expectations hardened further: traders priced a 69% chance of an October rate hike, while Fed officials continued emphasizing inflation risks. Source: Reuters/CME FedWatch, 2026-09-24.

Gold consequently reached its lowest level since September 16. Spot silver fell 1.3% to $63.60/oz in Reuters' 2:00 p.m. ET observation. Source: Reuters, 2026-09-24.

Alternative Opportunity

No clearly superior risk-reward opportunity is identified.

Cash and short-duration Treasury instruments have become more attractive as a defensive allocation because policy rates and market yields provide substantial carry without duration exposure. That is a liquidity/optionality choice rather than an asymmetric directional trade. Long-duration Treasuries remain vulnerable to inflation, supply and term-premium pressure; equities are increasingly exposed to the same discount-rate shock hurting metals.

Action Implied

Maintain correction and reduce tactical aggressiveness.

The secular evidence does not justify abandoning strategic metals exposure: ETF holdings and central-bank demand remain unusually strong, while fiscal financing pressure remains a long-run monetary-hedge argument. But those structural factors are currently losing the tactical contest against real yields, the dollar and Fed expectations.

New tactical exposure should require evidence of reversal rather than merely lower prices. The most persuasive repair would be a combination of falling real yields, a softer dollar, gold holding above the $4,235 support reference, and verified same-session silver/miner leadership.

A break below $4,235 with real yields still rising would materially increase the probability of a deeper correction toward the broader $4,000 area cited by Saxo Bank.
