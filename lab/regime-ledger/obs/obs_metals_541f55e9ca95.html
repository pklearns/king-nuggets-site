Regime: correction.
Direction: stabilizing.
Confidence: medium.

Gold composite: 30/80 available weight (futures positioning and price trend, relative strength, and miner confirmation unavailable).

Tactical and Secular Probabilities

Tactical 1--6 months: bull 23% / base 43% / bear 34%.

The tactical picture improved slightly from Thursday because real yields stopped accelerating and silver showed relative resilience. It remains unfavorable: real yields are still exceptionally high, the dollar remains firm, and another Fed hike is the market's base case.

Secular 12--24 months: bull 58% / base 28% / bear 14%.

The secular case remains positive because record gold ETF holdings, official-sector accumulation, fiscal financing pressure and silver's structural supply deficit have not been invalidated by the tactical correction.

What Changed

The principal improvement was in rates. The official 10-year real yield declined to 2.73% from 2.77% Thursday, while the 5-year real yield fell to 2.64% from 2.70%. The 30-year real yield remained 3.08%. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-25.

Nominal Treasury yields also eased modestly: the official 10-year par yield was 5.06%, versus 5.10% Thursday; the 30-year was 5.49%, versus 5.47% Thursday. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-25.

Bullion stabilized after the week's selloff. USAGOLD reported gold at $4,280.19/oz and silver at $64.04/oz earlier Friday; Fortune reported gold at $4,298/oz and silver at $65/oz at 9:00 a.m. ET. These are time-stamped observations from different sources rather than official closes and are not blended. Sources: USAGOLD and Fortune, 2026-09-25.

Macro / Flow / Positioning Evidence

Monetary policy: The Fed target range remains 3.75%-4.00% following the September increase. This is current state and remains fully score-eligible. Source: Federal Reserve, September 2026.

Real rates: Treasury's official September 25 real curve was 2.64% at 5 years, 2.73% at 10 years, 2.83% at 20 years and 3.08% at 30 years. Source: U.S. Treasury Daily Treasury Par Real Yield Curve Rates, 2026-09-25.

Rates expectations: Same-session market sources placed an October 25 bp hike around 64%-69%, with a December hike probability above 90% in one CME FedWatch report. The exact intraday probabilities varied with observation time, so no single percentage is treated as a closing level. Sources: CME FedWatch as reported by market sources, 2026-09-25.

Dollar: Same-session reports placed DXY around the 101 area and near an eight-week/two-month high. Because available sources differ on exact intraday levels, no exact DXY close is used as a scored price. Source: market reports, 2026-09-25.

ETF/fund flows: The latest valid monthly World Gold Council evidence remains strongly supportive: August global physically backed gold ETFs attracted $18 billion, added 121 tonnes, and reached a record 4,189 tonnes of holdings. Source: World Gold Council, September 2026, covering August. Separate September 25 reporting indicated small September 24 reductions at two large U.S. gold ETFs, which tempers but does not overturn the monthly signal.

Central banks: Latest valid periodic evidence remains strongly supportive for gold, including continued Chinese official accumulation in August. Source: World Gold Council, September 2026.

Futures positioning: UNAVAILABLE. The September 25 CFTC report was due after the close and should supersede the September 18 release, but the new gold and silver figures could not be independently verified. The prior September 15 positions are therefore not used in today's score.

Physical demand: High prices continue to constrain some price-sensitive gold demand. Silver retains a structural deficit, while the Silver Institute's 2026 outlook also incorporates weaker photovoltaic-related industrial demand through thrifting/substitution. Sources: World Gold Council and Silver Institute World Silver Survey 2026.

Fiscal/Treasury financing: The official 10-year nominal yield remained above 5% at 5.06% and the 30-year reached 5.49%. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-25. Persistent high financing costs and heavy sovereign borrowing remain a secular gold support but a tactical headwind through yields and the dollar.

Alternative Opportunity

No clearly superior liquid-market opportunity is identified.

Short-duration Treasury instruments and cash continue to offer attractive defensive carry and optionality, but they are not a superior asymmetric directional trade. Long-duration Treasuries remain exposed to inflation, fiscal supply and term-premium risk. Equities also remain sensitive to the same elevated discount rates pressuring metals.

Action Implied

Maintain correction, with direction upgraded from bearish to stabilizing.

Friday's slightly lower real yields and silver resilience are the first modest improvement after the midweek deterioration, but they do not establish a new trend. The dollar remains strong, Fed expectations remain restrictive, and full miner confirmation is unavailable.

Strategic metals exposure remains supported by ETF and central-bank demand. Tactical additions should still require evidence rather than simply lower prices: preferably a sustained reversal lower in real yields and the dollar together with verified same-session silver/miner leadership.

A gold break below the $4,235 support reference with real yields remaining near current levels would argue that the correction is extending. Conversely, a hold above that area followed by falling real yields and broad miner confirmation would materially improve the probability of an eventual bull-trend upgrade.

## Tactical and Secular Probabilities

**Tactical 1--6 months:** bull **23%** / base **43%** / bear **34%**.

The tactical picture improved slightly from Thursday because real yields
stopped accelerating and silver showed relative resilience. It remains
unfavorable: real yields are still exceptionally high, the dollar
remains firm, and another Fed hike is the market's base case.

**Secular 12--24 months:** bull **58%** / base **28%** / bear **14%**.

The secular case remains positive because record gold ETF holdings,
official-sector accumulation, fiscal financing pressure and silver's
structural supply deficit have not been invalidated by the tactical
correction.

## What Changed

The principal improvement was in rates. The official 10-year real yield
declined to **2.73%** from **2.77%** Thursday, while the 5-year real
yield fell to **2.64%** from **2.70%**. The 30-year real yield remained
**3.08%**. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-25.

Nominal Treasury yields also eased modestly: the official 10-year par
yield was **5.06%**, versus **5.10%** Thursday; the 30-year was
**5.49%**, versus **5.47%** Thursday. Source: U.S. Treasury, 2026-09-25.

Bullion stabilized after the week's selloff. USAGOLD reported gold at
**\$4,280.19/oz** and silver at **\$64.04/oz** earlier Friday; Fortune
reported gold at **\$4,298/oz** and silver at **\$65/oz** at 9:00 a.m.
ET. These are time-stamped observations from different sources rather
than official closes and are not blended. Sources: USAGOLD and Fortune,
2026-09-25.
