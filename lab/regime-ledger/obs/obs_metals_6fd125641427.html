Metals Regime Watch — 2026-09-08

Regime Snapshot

Regime: bull-trend
Direction: stable-to-slightly-weaker

The later report cutoff resolved the prior data-availability problem for this session: the U.S. Treasury published the September 8 real-yield curve, and attributable same-session closes were available for GDX, GDXJ, SIL, and SILJ.

Gold remained in a broader bull trend but failed to respond strongly to an unusually supportive geopolitical backdrop. Reuters reported spot gold at $4,385.09/oz and December gold futures settled at $4,430.10/oz on September 8. Silver was firmer, with Reuters reporting spot silver at $66.34/oz. At the same time, the U.S. Treasury reported the 10-year real yield at 2.43%, while Reuters reported markets pricing roughly a 60% probability of a September Fed rate hike. Those are material tactical headwinds.

Sources: Reuters, 2026-09-08; U.S. Department of the Treasury, 2026-09-08.

What Changed

The most important change was not a new bullish catalyst; it was confirmation that the restrictive macro backdrop persisted through the September 8 close.

The U.S. Treasury's September 8 real-yield curve showed the 10-year real yield at 2.43%, unchanged from September 4. Reuters reported that markets were pricing about a 60% probability of a 25 bp Fed hike at the September 15–16 meeting. Brent crude settled at $97.92/bbl according to Reuters, keeping energy-driven inflation risk elevated.

The dollar, however, softened. Investing.com displayed the DXY near 98.82, down roughly 0.34% on September 8. That offsets part of the rates pressure but has not been enough to restart gold acceleration.

Gold also received fresh structural support from China: official August data showed the PBoC added 650,000 troy ounces, roughly 20 tonnes, extending its buying streak to 22 months and lifting reported holdings to 76.73 million fine troy ounces.

Sources: U.S. Department of the Treasury, 2026-09-08; Reuters, 2026-09-08; Investing.com, 2026-09-08; PBoC/SAFE data reported 2026-09-07 and 2026-09-08.

Macro / Flow / Positioning Evidence

Monetary policy and real yields

The 10-year Treasury real yield was 2.43% on September 8. This is a direct same-session U.S. Treasury observation and therefore qualifies as a scored input. High real yields increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion.

Markets were pricing roughly a 60% chance of a September Fed rate hike, according to Reuters citing CME FedWatch. The combination of strong payrolls, rising oil prices and upcoming inflation data keeps the policy component materially restrictive.

Sources: U.S. Department of the Treasury, 2026-09-08; Reuters, 2026-09-08.

Dollar

Investing.com displayed the DXY at approximately 98.82, down about 0.34% on September 8. The WSJ Dollar Index also declined, although it is a different index and is not treated as a directly comparable DXY price.

Source: Investing.com, 2026-09-08; Wall Street Journal, 2026-09-08.

ETF and fund flows

The latest weekly LSEG Lipper data available at report time covered the week through September 2. Reuters reported that gold and other precious-metals funds attracted $2.85 billion, the eighth consecutive weekly inflow.

The latest World Gold Council monthly physically backed ETF report available for scoring showed $3 billion of global inflows in July, adding 23 tonnes and lifting holdings to 4,068 tonnes.

These releases have normal publication lags and are the latest available observations; no prior score was carried forward.

Sources: Reuters/LSEG Lipper, 2026-09-04; World Gold Council, 2026-08-11.

Central-bank and reserve demand

China's August reserve report showed a 650,000-ounce increase in official gold holdings, approximately 20 tonnes, extending the PBoC buying streak to 22 consecutive months. This is fresh evidence of official-sector demand even at elevated gold prices.

The latest broader World Gold Council quarterly data showed central banks bought 289 tonnes in Q2 2026. That quarterly figure is included as the latest broad official-sector benchmark, not as a same-day observation.

Sources: PBoC/SAFE data reported 2026-09-07; World Gold Council Gold Demand Trends Q2 2026, 2026-07-30.

CFTC positioning

The latest CFTC data are positions as of September 1, released on September 4. COMEX gold managed money was net long 136,771 contracts, down 7,976 week over week, with open interest at 415,196. Broader non-commercial gold positioning was net long 228,124 contracts, down 15,210 week over week.

Silver managed money was net long about 12,600 contracts, materially less stretched than gold, with open interest of 104,362.

The CFTC series is inherently weekly and lagged; September 1 is the latest available report and is therefore current for this component.

Source: CFTC Commitments of Traders, positions as of 2026-09-01.

Physical supply and demand

The World Gold Council's latest quarterly supply-and-demand report showed Q2 total gold demand, including OTC, at 1,269 tonnes, unchanged year over year. Bar-and-coin demand was 307 tonnes, while Q2 mine production rose 2% year over year to a record second-quarter 966 tonnes. Gold physical fundamentals are therefore constructive but not tight enough to override the macro headwind.

The Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 forecasts a sixth consecutive annual deficit of 46.3 million ounces. It also expects demand to remain above supply despite weaker industrial/PV consumption and firmer recycling and mine supply. The deficit and reliance on above-ground inventories remain constructive for silver, but the April forecast is the latest full-year survey rather than a same-day measure.

Sources: World Gold Council Gold Demand Trends Q2 2026, 2026-07-30; Silver Institute World Silver Survey 2026, 2026-04-15.

Fiscal and Treasury financing

Treasury's August borrowing estimate calls for $739 billion of privately held net marketable borrowing in the July–September quarter and $628 billion in October–December. Heavy financing needs remain a two-sided metals factor: they support longer-run concerns about debt, reserve diversification and term premium, but can also sustain high nominal and real yields tactically.

Source: U.S. Department of the Treasury, 2026-08-03.

Trend & Confirmation

Same-day miner closes were available for all four required ETFs, so this component is scored.

  • GDX: $98.41, down 0.86%.
  • GDXJ: $127.54, down 1.15%.
  • SIL: $98.50, down 0.80%.
  • SILJ: $31.69, up 0.03%.

Gold miners underperformed a session in which Reuters reported spot gold down about 0.4%, a mild negative confirmation signal. Silver itself was up about 0.3% by Reuters' afternoon report while SIL fell and SILJ was essentially flat, so silver-miner confirmation was also incomplete.

The larger miner trend has not broken: S&P Global Market Intelligence data show GDX at $76.05 on August 3 versus $98.41 on September 8, and GDXJ at $127.54 on September 8 after $122.28 on September 1. But the current session did not provide the leadership required for bull-acceleration.

Sources: S&P Global Market Intelligence via StockAnalysis.com, 2026-09-08; Reuters, 2026-09-08.

Trend conclusion: continuing bull trend, currently consolidating rather than accelerating.

Silver confirmation: partial. Silver held up better than gold, but the silver-miner complex did not broadly confirm.

Failed-breakout warning: present but not confirmed. Gold's inability to rally on escalating Middle East risk while real yields remain high is a warning; the evidence is not yet sufficient to classify the regime as correction.

Source: World Gold Council Gold ETF Flows, February 2026 report.

Staged de-risking signals

Do not require the January highs to be retested. Staged de-risking becomes materially more compelling if several of these occur together:

  • renewed rapid CFTC long accumulation after the recent partial washout;
  • fund inflows accelerate from already-strong levels;
  • silver begins a vertical move while miners fail to confirm;
  • miners diverge negatively while bullion establishes fresh highs;
  • daily ranges and trading volumes expand sharply;
  • repeated attempts at new highs fail within a short window.

At present, that cluster is not present.

Alternative Opportunity

No clearly superior liquid-market opportunity is identified.

Oil has stronger immediate momentum, but Reuters reported Brent settling at $97.92/bbl on September 8 amid direct Middle East supply risk. That produces substantial gap and headline risk rather than an obviously superior entry profile. Long-duration Treasuries offer high yields but remain exposed to inflation, fiscal supply and term-premium pressure. Equities remain vulnerable to the same rates pressure affecting metals.

Source for Brent price: Reuters, 2026-09-08.

Action Implied

Maintain strategic exposure; do not treat the current setup as bull-acceleration.

The current evidence argues for patience rather than either aggressive chasing or wholesale de-risking:

  • The bull-trend classification survives because flows, central-bank demand, silver physical fundamentals and the broader price structure remain constructive.
  • The regime is not bull-acceleration because real yields remain high, a September Fed hike is credible, gold is not responding strongly to geopolitical stress, and miners are not providing leadership.
  • A correction call would require stronger evidence that the broader trend is failing rather than merely consolidating.
  • Bull-excess de-risking should be triggered by a cluster of renewed crowding, vertical silver action, miner divergence, extreme flow acceleration and failed highs—not by proximity to a historical price alone.

The next major regime catalysts are U.S. PPI, U.S. CPI and the September 15–16 FOMC decision.
