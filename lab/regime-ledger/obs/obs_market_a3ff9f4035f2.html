Risks and Contradictions

The primary conflict is strong relative performance by equal-weight and defensive sector ETFs versus weak absolute index momentum and filtered-stock participation. This favors a defensive classification without asserting that every equity group is deteriorating. Year-to-date gains remain positive, so a long-horizon bear-market label is not established by these observations alone.

Data-quality boundaries: (1) broad-stock A/D and highs/lows are vendor-filtered rather than exchange-certified; (2) sector ETF performance and a broad-stock sector aggregation use different universes; (3) SPY daily return differs materially between The Daily Tape and Gainbot and is excluded from scoring; (4) Micron's reported close and daily return differ between those same vendors and are excluded from scoring; (5) oil daily change differs between Reuters and The Daily Tape across benchmarks/timestamps and is excluded; (6) same-day HY OAS and ACM term premium are unavailable, and the latest valid prior-day daily HY OAS is not relabeled current. None of these disputed levels is a scored input.

Educational Summary

A Defensive market regime ordinarily describes a market in which near-term risk pricing, leadership and participation are deteriorating faster than the longer-term trend has fully reversed. Here, the sharp volatility increase and persistent technology/semiconductor weakness support that reading; relative resilience in equal-weight equities, staples, utilities and energy argues against describing the decline as universal. Elevated yields, still-positive economic growth and strong latest-quarter AI-supplier earnings explain why the equity selloff does not by itself establish recession, fiscal dominance or failure of AI investment returns. This is an environmental interpretation, not an action recommendation.
