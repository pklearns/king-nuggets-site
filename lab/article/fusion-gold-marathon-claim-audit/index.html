Fusion Gold Claim Audit

A fusion start-up says it turned mercury into stable gold at ten times CERN’s record. I checked it one stage at a time: the science, the engineering, the economics, and the market.

01The announcement

[PATRICK WRITES — section 01: The announcement]

02Making gold is not a gold business

The physics is not in dispute. A 14 MeV neutron from a deuterium–tritium fusion reaction can knock one neutron out of mercury-198. That leaves mercury-197, which decays to stable gold-197 with a half-life of 64.1 hours.

The record Marathon Fusion says it beat belongs to CERN. In May 2025 the ALICE collaboration reported that about 86 billion gold nuclei were created across the LHC’s four main experiments during Run 2, 2015 to 2018. That is 29 picograms, and the nuclei fragmented almost immediately. CERN made gold. It kept none.

Marathon’s post on 21 October is a 104-second video and three sentences. It says that last month the company “demonstrated high-rate alchemy … beating CERN’s record by a factor of 10.” It gives no mass, no definition of “10x” and no paper. Press coverage describes the evidence in the thread as a single gamma-ray spectrum.

Ten times what? Read as total mass, ten times CERN is 290 picograms, under a third of a nanogram. Read as a rate, the total depends on a run time Marathon has not disclosed. Either way, nothing disclosed comes anywhere near a quantity you could weigh.

03The economics are a simulation

The tonnage comes from a 2025 preprint by Marathon’s team (arXiv 2507.13461). It models about 2 tonnes of gold per gigawatt of thermal power per year. Its reference case is a 1.5 GWth tokamak that makes 2,953 kg a year. It holds 622 tonnes of mercury-198 enriched to 90%, and burns 0.47% of that inventory a year.

Thermal and electric gigawatts are different units. GWth is the heat a plant makes, and GWe is the electricity it sells. At the paper’s assumed 40% conversion, 2 tonnes per GWth is 5 tonnes per GWe. The “five tonnes per gigawatt” figure in circulation is the same claim in different units.

Against 2025 mine supply of 3,672 tonnes (World Gold Council), 2 tonnes is 0.054%. The paper’s whole reference reactor is 0.080%.

The gold also comes out radioactive. The paper puts the wait at 6.8 years to reach the least hazardous low-level-waste class, 13.7 years to drop below the NRC labelling threshold, and 17.7 years to be less active than a banana. There is no published cost per ounce. The paper values gold at $100,000/kg and models the revenue, not what it costs to make.

04The technology is still changing

In April 2026 two Marathon researchers posted a second paper (arXiv 2604.02590). At very high neutron flux, around 1016 neutrons per cm² per second, heavier mercury isotopes step down to gold through chains of reactions. Natural mercury then works, and enrichment becomes optional. Enriched feed still produces about three times more gold. The mercury blanket shrinks from about 390 tonnes at today’s magnetic-confinement flux to about 4 tonnes.

The paper reports 30-year totals, so I converted them to annual rates. At high flux, a 1 GWth source makes about 0.5 tonnes a year on natural mercury and 1.5 on enriched. At magnetic-confinement flux, natural mercury gives about 0.2 tonnes a year. The feedstock limit loosens, and the binding constraint becomes how many reactors exist and how many neutrons they make.

None of this is validated. Inertial-confinement plants with high-flux blankets do not exist. The flow-through blanket is a design sketch in the supplement, and I found no cooling or activation analysis. The paper also puts the world’s extractable mercury at 1.5 million tonnes. Karl Lackner, emeritus director at the Max Planck Institute for Plasma Physics, told Max Planck Forschung (3/2025) that all the mercury in the Earth’s crust is estimated at about 600,000 tonnes. The two figures are two and a half times apart and probably use different definitions. I can’t reconcile them from the sources.

05The gold market: flows against stocks

Gold is priced against what already exists, not just what is mined. The World Gold Council estimates 219,891 tonnes above ground at the end of 2025. Mine supply adds about 1.7% a year to that stock. One gigawatt of modeled fusion would add 0.0009%.

The Council’s Qaurum model suggests that, holding everything else constant, a supply change of about 25 tonnes moves the price about 1%. By that rule of thumb, the reference reactor’s 2.95 tonnes is about 0.1%. Treat that as illustrative only, since the model was not built for this question.

The post landed at 20:14 UTC on 21 October, after the US cash close. On 22 October, the first full session, gold futures fell 2.7%, silver 4.9%, platinum 3.4%, GDX 5.8% and GDXJ 6.8%, while WTI crude rose 2.0%. Coverage tied the day to oil reviving rate-hike bets ahead of the Fed meeting on 27–28 October. Silver falling harder than gold fits that macro story, not a gold-specific shock. One confounded day is not an event study, and no distinct market response to the announcement can be established.

06What would change my view

  • A gold mass and a cost per ounce, with an independent assay.
  • Peer review of either paper.
  • Tonne-scale mercury-198 enrichment, or a demonstrated high-flux flow-through blanket.
  • A D-T plant operating with a mercury blanket and a tritium breeding ratio above 1.1.
  • A ruling under the Minamata Convention on mercury as fusion feedstock. The 2026 paper notes that the Convention prohibits new mercury mining.
